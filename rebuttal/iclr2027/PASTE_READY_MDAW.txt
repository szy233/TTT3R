Thank you. Your points hold. All runs share one checkpoint. Tests are paired by sequence (Wilcoxon, bootstrap 95% CI, no multiplicity correction). "Better" needs p<0.05 and a gap above 2.2%, and anything else is "tied", which does not show equivalence. A 1e-7 change to one multiplier (one draw) moved means by up to 4.7% (TUM ATE), 6.9% (KITTI ATE) and 13.4% (KITTI segment rotation error), so smaller gaps are within numerical noise (revised PDF, A.12).

**W2, Q1. γ.** The code uses w = ē^γ and α∥(t) = w·α⊥ + (1-w)·α∥, where ē is a per-token EMA of e_t that Eq. 12 omits. So, as you say, small γ is isotropic and large γ approaches ortho. A.4 and the Table 5 caption state the reverse and are corrected in the revision (Table 6 there). On ScanNet, ATE rises from 0.327 at γ=0.5 to 0.456 at γ=5, moving from const 0.5 (0.283) toward ortho (0.488). γ=0 is an off switch that runs ortho, the large-γ limit, so the family is continuous only for γ>0. Outside the γ sweep, all ortho results use γ=0.

**W2, Q2. EMA.** The code, Eq. 7 and Algorithm 1 all update the direction before projecting, but only the code renormalizes it, and all numbers come from the code. At β_ema=0 the reference equals the current delta and the orthogonal part vanishes, so the A.8 setup was wrong. The revision fixes Eq. 7, Algorithm 1, A.8 and Figure 2(c), which showed d_{t-1}. Projecting onto d_{t-1} instead gives TUM 0.0616 vs 0.0545 for the updated direction (updated better, 8/8, p=0.0078) and ScanNet 0.4764 vs 0.4883 (tied, p=0.84).

**Q2. Brake.** It is not an exact special case of Eq. 6. It fits only if α⊥=α∥=g varies per token and frame, and g uses the previous delta, not the EMA direction. It is a related scalar baseline, and the revision removes "strictly generalizes" from the abstract and introduction. The paper prints τ=2, but the TUM, ScanNet and ARKitScenes results use τ=1. A true τ=2 gives TUM 0.082 and ScanNet 0.311, tied with const 0.5 (0.079, 0.283). The submitted KITTI Odometry column used τ=2, and the revision reruns it at τ=1.

What stands: training-free scalar dampening lowers ATE against TTT3R at 1000 frames (brake, set on these benchmarks; 8/8 on TUM, 54/65 on ScanNet, both p<0.01), at higher mean RPE rotation. On ARKitScenes at 500 frames brake ties TTT3R (p=0.29). We keep decomposition as a separately tested control axis, not as a general improvement.
